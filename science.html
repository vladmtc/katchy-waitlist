Why one question works

Everyone's mind wanders in lectures. What matters is how fast you get back.

In a 2013 study in PNAS, Szpunar, Khan and Schacter had students watch a recorded lecture. Students who answered short questions between segments reported their minds wandering about half as often (roughly 19% vs 41%), took more notes and remembered more on the final test.

Katchy uses the same idea at the exact moment you drift: a short recap so you can rejoin, then one retrieval question, because actively recalling something makes it stick far better than re-reading it.

Honest caveat: that study was a lab experiment with a 21-minute recorded lecture and questions at fixed times. We're now testing whether the effect holds in real classrooms, with questions you trigger yourself.
